This is about your paper "A space of inference spaces in the space 
sciences", https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06078. I downloaded the reference 
outputs linked in the README of 
JohannesBuchner/space-of-inference-spaces and regenerated Table 1 with 
evaluateproblems.py.

The archive on the MPE server is cut off. It is 3,746,656,851 bytes 
long, gzip reports an unexpected end of file, and the listing stops 
inside asymgauss-100d. I fetched the last megabyte a second time and it 
is identical, so the file ends that way on the server. It holds 
complete outputs for 23 of the 60 problems in problems.txt. 
asymgauss-100d lacks its points.hdf5, and the remaining toy problems 
are missing entirely, among them all 24 spike-and-slab variants.

For the 23 problems that are there, six of the eight columns come out 
identical to the last printed digit: dimensionality, cost, depth, 
width, inequality and phase transition.

The other two columns change from run to run. The number of modes and 
the non-Gaussianity are both computed from the equally weighted 
samples, and resample_equal draws those without a fixed seed. For 
exo-rv-3, three runs gave 9, 6 and 15 modes, and the Results section 
builds on the 9 ("Fitting for 3 exoplanets yields 9 modes"). Five runs 
gave a non-Gaussianity between 0.05 and 0.16 for the same problem, 
where Table 1 has 0.06. Your joblib cache keeps the first draw on your 
machine, which is presumably why it never showed. With 
np.random.seed(0) set before the run, two runs from an empty cache 
agreed in all eight columns on the four problems I tried.

The non-Gaussianity has a second issue. 
compute_gaussian_approximation_loss evaluates the Gaussian at 
samples[i] but pairs it with the weight and logL of weighted point i. 
Because samples[i] is a resampled copy, it is never point i itself. 
Every sample is an exact copy of a weighted point, so each can be given 
its own logL. Done that way, the value is stable across seeds and moves 
in several rows, for sample-dist-gauss from 0.08 to 0.13 and for 
exo-rv-3 from 0.06 to 0.13.

Two small things besides. In two rows the paper and the committed 
evaluateproblems.tex disagree on the non-Gaussianity. For 
sample-dist-hist the paper prints 0.12 and the file has 0.08, and my 
runs give about 0.09. For rosenbrock-20d the paper prints 0.09 and the 
file has 1.27, which I could not recompute because that problem is 
missing from the archive. The other 58 rows of Table 1 match the 
committed file exactly. And lines 37 and 38 of the README swap the two 
posteriorstacker folders. problems.txt has them right, since flex11 is 
the one with eleven bin parameters.
